# 8. The all-sets playthrough

> Wear whatever is best right now, keep skills that work in any of it, and pick a school when the Grandmaster sets arrive.

## The idea

The six school chapters that follow each build one school from White Orchard to Toussaint. Many players don't play that way: they hunt every witcher set, wear whichever is strongest at the moment, and only settle on a school late in the game. This chapter is the build for that playthrough. It follows the same layout as the school chapters, with the reset in place of a final layout.

It rests on three facts from Part I:

- **Sets give no bonus before Grandmaster** (chapter 3). Until level 40, a set is only its stats and its weight class, so switching sets costs you nothing.
- **School Techniques follow weight, not sets** (chapter 3). Wolf, Griffin, Manticore and Viper count any medium armor, Cat counts light and Bear counts heavy.
- **Respec is cheap** (chapter 21). A Potion of Clearance from Keira Metz costs about 1,000 crowns and refunds every skill point.

So the plan buys the skills that most of the six school plans share, adds Wolf School Techniques for the medium armor you'll wear most of the time, and leaves each school's own skills for one reset, once you know which Grandmaster set you'll finish in.

## How it plays

A sword fighter who keeps every Sign useful. Quen keeps you alive, and its held form heals you from what it absorbs. Fast attacks after a dodge and strong attacks after fast attacks do the damage. Yrden's Magic Trap handles wraiths and crowds, and Igni strips armor so the sword hits harder. Razor Focus starts every fight with an Adrenaline point, and Undying spends the bar to bring you back when a hit would kill you. It doesn't depend on a set, a weight class or a missable item, which is the point.

**The shared core.** These are the skills the six school plans in this book buy most often by 28 points:

| Skill | School plans that buy it | What it gives every build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exploding Shield → Active Shield | 6 of 6 | Quen, and a held shield that heals you from what it absorbs |
| Muscle Memory | 5 of 6 | Sword damage after a dodge |
| Three Strikes → Razor Focus | 5 of 6 | One Adrenaline point at the start of every fight |
| Refreshment | 5 of 6 | Healing from every potion |
| Hunter Instinct | 5 of 6 | Mostly the way to Acquired Tolerance |
| Strength Training | 4 of 6 | Strong-attack damage |
| Undying | 4 of 6 | A second life every 30 seconds |
| Acquired Tolerance | 4 of 6 | Room for decoctions |

Two picks come from Griffin and Wolven instead: **Magic Trap**, because wraiths turn up in every region and Yrden is the answer to them, and **Melt Armor**, because Igni's armor reduction makes your sword hit harder too. Delusion is in for Axii's dialogue options; skip it if you don't use them.

## Skills by phase

Buy each table from the top down; every prerequisite is either already owned or listed higher up. The point total in a phase heading is the gate, not its level range: purchases and slots both run on **Ability Points earned**, and the level estimates assume about one point per level. Each Place of Power gives a point the first time you use it, the Magic Acorn two and Blood and Wine's Golden Egg one (chapter 20), so a thorough player reaches each total several levels early.

### Phase 4: level 30+ until the reset

- **Rank up what you slot:** Active Shield 3 (no Stamina drain at all), Refreshment, Acquired Tolerance, Magic Trap 3, Razor Focus 3.

- **Hunter Instinct earns a slot** only if you wear crit gear: Feline pieces, crit swords or the Katakan decoction.

## Slots, mutagens and mutation

**While slots are scarce,** equip in this order and fill new slots from the top of what's left. You have 4 slots after the opening, 7 by the end of Phase 1, 9 by the end of Phase 2, 10 at 22 points and 11 by the end of Phase 3; the 12th opens at 30 Ability Points (chapter 2).

1. Muscle Memory
2. Razor Focus
3. Exploding Shield, then Active Shield once you have it
4. Undying
5. Strength Training
6. Magic Trap
7. Wolf School Techniques (once you're in medium armor)
8. Melt Armor
9. Delusion

**At 9 slots (from 18 points):**

| Group | Skills | Mutagen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top left | Muscle Memory, Strength Training, Razor Focus | Red |
| Top right | Active Shield, Magic Trap, Melt Armor | Blue |
| Bottom left | Undying, Wolf School Techniques, Delusion | Blue |
| Bottom right | Locked until 22 points | — |

**The 10th slot, at 22 points,** is the bottom-right group's first, and the other two arrive at 26 and 30 points (chapter 2). Refreshment, bought in Phase 2, is the skill for it — unless you skipped Delusion, in which case Refreshment is already in the bottom left and this slot takes the free-slot pick below.

**The full layout (from 30 points):**

| Group | Skills | Mutagen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top left | Muscle Memory, Strength Training, Razor Focus | Red |
| Top right | Active Shield, Magic Trap, Melt Armor | Blue |
| Bottom left | Undying, Wolf School Techniques, Delusion | Blue |
| Bottom right | Refreshment, Acquired Tolerance, Survival Instinct | Green |

Three Strikes, Sustained Glyphs, Exploding Shield and Sun and Stars stay unslotted.

**Without Delusion,** buy Refreshment in its place in Phase 1 and slot it in the bottom left with a green mutagen. The bottom right then has a free slot: Hunter Instinct if you wear crit gear, otherwise Three Strikes, whose small bonus to a fourth same-style hit beats an empty slot (chapter 20).

**Mutation:** none until the reset. If Blood and Wine comes first, research the mutation of the school you're heading for (chapter 5) and slot its skills when you rebuild.

## Consumables and the fight loop

**Potions:** Swallow for healing, which Refreshment adds to, Thunderbolt for attack power and Tawny Owl for Stamina. **Oils:** always the one that matches the target; its bonus applies to every hit. **Decoction:** once Acquired Tolerance gives you room, one at a time; Forktail suits a build that mixes Signs, fast and strong attacks (chapters 4 and 12).

**The loop:**

1. **Quen first,** and hold it (Active Shield) when you're surrounded.
2. **Magic Trap against wraiths and crowds.** Lay it where the enemies will come to you.
3. **Igni the armored ones** so Melt Armor softens them for your sword.
4. **Dodge, then fast attacks,** for Muscle Memory; finish the string with a strong attack for Strength Training.
5. **Hold your Adrenaline.** Razor Focus gives you the first point, and Undying needs at least one to save you.

## Gear path

Wear whichever set has the best stats; before level 40 the only thing a change can break is your Technique. Check the weight of each piece you put on:

| You put on | Sets on the way | Technique | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Medium | Nilfgaardian (10), Griffin (11, 18, 26, 34), Wolven (14, 21, 29, 34), Forgotten Wolven (20, 34), Viper armor (39), Manticore (40) | Wolf School Techniques | Nothing |
| Light | Temerian (4), Feline (17, 23, 29, 34) | Cat School Techniques: +2% fast attack damage per light piece per rank | Buy it (1 point, no prerequisite) and slot it in Wolf's place |
| Heavy | Undvik (16), Ursine (20, 25, 30, 34) | Bear School Techniques: +2% maximum Vitality and +2% strong attack damage per heavy piece per rank | Buy it (1 point, no prerequisite) and slot it in Wolf's place |

Levels are each tier's required level to wear (chapter 3), not when the set becomes available; Wolven's first diagrams wait at Kaer Morhen, which opens after *Ugly Baby*, so that set arrives mid-game rather than at level 14. Chapter 16 has every set's diagram locations, region by region.

- **A Technique counts pieces, not sets.** Mixing a Feline chest with medium gloves, trousers and boots still leaves three pieces for Wolf.
- **Weight also changes Stamina.** Light armor refills Stamina 25% faster and heavy 25% slower in combat (chapter 20), which you'll feel in how often you can cast and dodge.
- **The Balance glyphword makes all your armor count as medium** (chapter 6), so with Hearts of Stone you can wear any set's stats and keep Wolf School Techniques.
- **Points in the old Technique sit idle** until you switch back. At one to three points that's cheaper than a respec.
- **Swords:** whatever hits hardest. Keep a matching oil on both.

## The maths

**Undying** (file-verified, chapter 20) sets your Vitality to this share of its maximum when a hit would kill you:

| Adrenaline held | Rank 1 | Rank 2 | Rank 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (fight start, with Razor Focus) | 10% | 43% | 77% |
| 2 | 20% | 53% | 87% |
| 3 (full bar) | 30% | 63% | 97% |

Rank 2 with one point beats rank 1 with a full bar, which is why this plan buys it at 13 points.

**Hunter Instinct** adds crit damage to sword hits only, and only with a full Adrenaline bar, the oil that matches the target and an actual crit. It adds 0.2 to the crit bonus per rank, so at Geralt's base 5% crit chance rank 1 raises your average sword damage by at most about 1% (chapter 20's crit formula). That's why it's a stepping stone here and a core skill in Feline.

**Wolf School Techniques** at rank 3 in four medium pieces is +24% weapon damage and +24% Sign intensity, the only Technique that boosts both (chapter 3).

## The reset

Reset once, when you know which set you'll finish in:

- **At level 40**, when every Grandmaster set, the full Manticore set included, comes from Lazare Lafargue in Toussaint (chapter 16). From then on the set bonuses decide a build.
- **At the start of Blood and Wine**, if you want to plan around a mutation from the beginning (chapter 5).

How to do it:

1. Pick the school whose Grandmaster set you'll wear. Chapter 7 compares them and chapter 15 ranks them.
2. Read that school's chapter: its Phase 4, its finished layout, its mutation.
3. Buy a Potion of Clearance from Keira Metz (about 1,000 crowns) and drink it. It refunds skill points; points spent on mutations need the Potion of Restoration (chapter 21).
4. Buy the whole school plan from its first table down, in one sitting, and slot its finished layout.

You lose less than it looks: Quen, Razor Focus and Undying are in nearly every school's plan, so you'll buy most of them straight back.

## Verdict

**Strengths.** Every point stays useful through every set change, and Quen plus Undying make it safe from level 13. Nothing in it is missable, it needs no expansion, and it teaches you every tool before you commit to one.

**Weaknesses.** It's a generalist by design: it never takes the multipliers that make a school strong, such as Griffin's Catalyst or Feline's Battle Frenzy, so a committed school pulls ahead from the mid-game on. A switch to light or heavy armor costs a point or two for the other Technique.

**Who it suits:** players who collect every set, first playthroughs, and anyone who wants to decide on a school after seeing them all.
